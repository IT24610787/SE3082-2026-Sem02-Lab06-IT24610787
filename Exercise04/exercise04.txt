--------

Explanation
-----------
Part 1: `#pragma omp parallel for`
  A team of threads is created, and the iterations of the loop directly
  after the pragma are shared out among them. That is only the OUTER loop
  (r = 1..5), i.e. 5 iterations. Each thread gets some of the r values and
  runs the ENTIRE inner loop (k = 1..10) for each r it owns. So all 10 lines
  for a given r come from the same thread.
  Consequence: at most 5 threads get any work. With 8 threads, 3 threads
  are idle. With 4 threads, one thread gets 2 values of r, so the work is
  uneven (20 vs 10 lines).


Part 2: `#pragma omp parallel for collapse(2)`
  collapse(2) merges the two nested loops into ONE iteration space of
  5 x 10 = 50 (r, k) pairs, and those 50 iterations are shared among the
  threads. Now all threads can be used (with 8 threads each gets about 6-7
  iterations), the load is more even, and the 10 iterations of one r value
  may be split across different threads.
  Requirement: the loops must be perfectly nested (nothing between the two
  `for` statements), and the inner loop bounds must not depend on the outer
  loop variable.

Summary: without collapse the parallelism is limited to 5 iterations; with
collapse it is 50, which scales to more threads.
